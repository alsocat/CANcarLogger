OBD logger · rev C (5 V transceiver, sleep)

CAN logger wiring

Always-on power from OBD pin 16 through a 1 A fuse and a fixed 5 V Mini560 into an ESP32-S3 SuperMini. A CAN transceiver, powered from 5 V, connects the S3 to the diagnostic CAN bus on pins 6 and 14; its receive line is divided down for the S3. A battery-voltage divider lets the board sleep while the car is off. Pin labels are drawn as they are printed on each board, not where they sit physically.

22–24 AWG is plenty for every wire here. The whole board draws well under half an amp.

Build order

  1. Check the Mini560 on the benchFeed it 12 V and measure OUT+ to OUT−. It should read about 5.0 V. A mislabeled 9 V or 12 V module would destroy the S3.
  2. Prep the boardsRemove the “121” resistor from the transceiver and the red power LED from the SuperMini.
  3. Check the CRX dividerPower the transceiver from 5 V with the divider built but not yet on GPIO 5. The junction should read 2.9–3.4 V.
  4. Solder the harnessFollow the wire list. Heat-shrink every joint and twist CAN-H and CAN-L together.
  5. Flash over USB-CDo this with the harness unplugged from the car, so the computer and the Mini560 never power the board at the same time.
  6. Plug into the carWith the ignition on, the status page should show “tx ok” climbing and frames received.
  7. Let sleep arm itselfThe board only starts sleeping once its battery reading matches the engine computer's. The status page says when.
  8. Measure sleep currentPut a multimeter in series with the 12 V wire after the car locks and goes quiet. Under 3 mA is the target.
Before plugging in
  • CANH and CANL swapped won't damage anything, but the board will see no traffic.
  • CRX swings to about 3.7 V on these boards; never connect it to GPIO 5 without the divider.
  • A genuine 3.3 V SN65HVD230 would go on the 3V3 pin with CRX direct instead.
  • Don't power the board from USB and the car at the same time.
